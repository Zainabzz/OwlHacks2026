# Weather Walk

> **Built for OwlHacks 2026** — Temple University's annual 24-hour hackathon.

Weather Walk is a weather-aware, environment-informed walking navigation app for Philadelphia. Instead of just finding the _fastest_ path, it compares up to three Mapbox walking routes and ranks them by a combination of travel time and real environmental conditions — sun exposure, rain likelihood, wind headwind impact, and geometric building shade — fetched at estimated arrival times along the route.

The app runs on **iOS, Android, and the web** from a single React Native / Expo codebase, backed by a Python FastAPI server that orchestrates all spatial and weather data in parallel.

## Features

- **Up to 3 distinct walking routes** per request, generated by Mapbox Directions with detour fallbacks when fewer alternatives exist
- **Environment-aware ranking** — routes are sorted by time, with a shade/coolness preference in warm weather and a sunshine preference in cold weather
- **Arrival-time weather forecasting** — 12 equally-spaced route sections each receive their own Open-Meteo hourly forecast keyed to when you'll actually be walking that segment
- **Current conditions averaged along routes** — dual-provider (Open-Meteo + OpenWeather) temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity, and AQI with trapezoidal distance-weighting
- **Geometric building shade** — flat-roof prism shadow geometry computed from Philadelphia's official building footprint dataset using real solar position (azimuth + elevation via `astral`)
- **Philadelphia street tree proximity** — count of 2025 inventory trees within 10 m of each route
- **Cross-platform** — renders on iOS with `react-native-maps`, on Android/web with Leaflet in a WebView
- **Dark mode** with per-screen persistence

## How It Works — Technically

### Route Generation & Ranking

`POST /api/routes` accepts `{ start: {latitude, longitude}, destination: {latitude, longitude} }` and calls the **Mapbox Directions API** with a walking profile at a conservative pace of **1.30 m/s** (vs. Mapbox's default 1.42 m/s). The backend requests up to three alternatives; when fewer are returned, it fires **eight nearby waypoint detour requests in parallel** (3-second timeout each) and merges any unique paths. Duplicates and detours more than 2.2× the direct distance are discarded.

Each route is then ranked with a composite score:

- **Primary key**: travel time (ascending)
- **Weather modifier** (warm/mild dry weather): bonus for routes with higher predicted shade or canopy coverage
- **Weather modifier** (cold dry weather): bonus for routes with more sun exposure
- Forecast rain disables the dry-weather modifier entirely

Exactly one route is marked `isPreferred: true`; `rank` starts at 1.

### Weather Sampling Along Routes

`services/route_weather.py` samples **5 equally spaced points** along each route geometry (endpoints at half weight — trapezoidal integration). For each sample it calls both weather providers in parallel:

- **Open-Meteo** — free, keyless, preferred source
- **OpenWeather** `/data/2.5/weather` — paid fallback

Provider results are normalized to a common schema, then distance-weighted averaged to produce a single `weather` object per route with `temperatureF`, `feelsLikeF`, `windSpeedMph`, rain/snow depth, cloud cover, AQI, and a `coveragePercent` indicating how much of the route had valid data.

All numeric conversions (°C→°F, km/h→mph, mm→inches) are performed explicitly on the backend; the client never sees raw metric units.

### Arrival-Time Exposure Forecasting

`services/exposure.py` takes a route and its expected walking duration, then:

1. **Divides the route into 12 equal segments** in EPSG:32618 (UTM 18N) using Shapely's `substring`
2. **Computes an estimated arrival timestamp** for each segment midpoint based on a steady walking pace
3. **Batches all 12 forecast requests** into a single Open-Meteo multi-location hourly request (12 lat/lon pairs, `direct_normal_irradiance`, `rain`, `showers`, `wind_speed_10m`, `wind_direction_10m`)
4. **Looks up the correct forecast hour** — accumulations use the hour ending _after_ arrival; instantaneous wind uses the nearest hour
5. **Aggregates three metrics** per route:
   - `sunExposurePercent` — % of walking time with DNI ≥ 120 W/m² (nighttime auto-zero via `astral` solar elevation)
   - `rainExposurePercent` — % of walking time in hours where rain + showers ≥ 0.1 mm
   - `windImpact` — average positive headwind component in mph, computed from forecast wind direction vs. each segment's walking bearing (weighted by sub-segment length)

Each metric independently tracks `coveragePercent` so partial forecast outages don't silently pollute averages.

### Geometric Building Shade

`spatial/shade.py` implements a **flat-roof prism shadow** algorithm:

1. Solar azimuth and elevation are computed via `astral` for 12 arrival-time samples along the route
2. The **SQLite RTree index** (`spatial/buildings.py`) is queried for all building footprints within a bounding box of the route, reproduced from Philadelphia's [LI Building Footprints](https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/building-footprints/) dataset (474 MB GeoJSON → EPSG:32618)
3. For each footprint, a shadow polygon is extruded: `shadow_length = height / tan(solar_elevation)`, then the footprint and a translated copy are joined with wall-face quads via `shapely.unary_union`
4. `canopy.py` clips each shadow to the route LineString and unions the fragments to compute a **shade coverage percentage** without double-counting overlapping shadows
5. When valid building shade is available, `exposure.py`'s `apply_shade()` multiplies the open-sky sun estimate by the unshaded fraction

Heights use `approx_hgt` (preferred) or `max_hgt` from the dataset, converted from feet to meters. Footprints missing both fields are omitted.

### Tree Inventory Spatial Index

`spatial/trees.py` builds a **SQLite RTree index** from Philadelphia's 2025 PPR Tree Inventory GeoJSON (point data). At query time, the route is buffered by 10 m, candidate tree trunks are fetched via a bounding-box RTree query, then each candidate's precise Shapely point distance is verified against the route LineString.

The index is stamped with the source file's mtime and size; a mismatch triggers an automatic rebuild. A threading lock ensures concurrent requests don't race during the build.

> **Note**: The dataset contains trunk point locations only — no canopy outlines — so tree _shade_ coverage is unavailable. Only a trunk count within 10 m of the route is reported.

### Address Search

Place search is powered by the **Mapbox Search Box API** (`/search/searchbox/v1/suggest` + `/retrieve`). Suggest and retrieve calls share a session token generated per search interaction to consolidate billing. The token-bearing upstream URL is never forwarded to the client; only sanitized suggestion text and coordinates are returned.

## Setup

### Prerequisites

- **Python 3.11+**
- **Node.js 18+** and npm
- **Git**
- A shell that can run `.sh` scripts (Git Bash, WSL, or macOS/Linux Terminal)

### Backend

```sh
# 1. Create the virtual environment (from the repo root)
python3 -m venv backend/.venv

# 2. Install dependencies
backend/.venv/bin/python -m pip install -r backend/requirements.txt

# 3. Create and populate the backend environment file
#    (see Environment Variables section below)
```

### Spatial Data

The app uses two Philadelphia open datasets. Download them and place them at the paths shown:

| File                              | Source                                                                                           | Destination path              |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| `LI_BUILDING_FOOTPRINTS.geojson`  | [OpenDataPhilly — Building Footprints](https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/building-footprints/) | `backend/data/raw/buildings/` |
| `ppr_tree_inventory_2025.geojson` | [OpenDataPhilly — PPR Tree Inventory](https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/ppr-tree-inventory/)   | `backend/data/raw/trees/`     |

The `start.sh` launcher automatically builds SQLite RTree indexes from these files before the API starts. Indexes are stored in `backend/data/processed/` (gitignored). If the building footprint file is absent, the server still starts — building shade metrics will simply be unavailable.

### Starting the Server

```sh
# From the repository root:
sh backend/start.sh --reload
```

The API will be available at `http://localhost:8000`. Visit `/health` to confirm it is running.

### Frontend

```sh
cd frontend/mobile

# Install dependencies
npm install

# Create the environment file
echo "EXPO_PUBLIC_API_URL=http://localhost:8000" > .env

# Start the web dev server
npm run web

# Or for native (iOS/Android via Expo Go):
npm start
```

### Running on a Physical Device

On a physical phone using Expo Go, the device must reach the backend over your LAN:

```sh
# Find your computer's LAN IP address (e.g. 192.168.1.42)
# Set it in frontend/mobile/.env:
EXPO_PUBLIC_API_URL=http://192.168.1.42:8000

# Restart Expo after changing the variable:
cd frontend/mobile && npx expo start --clear
```

## Known Limitations

- **Philadelphia only.** The supported geographic area is strictly bounded: longitude −75.30 to −74.95, latitude 39.85 to 40.15. Requests outside this bounding box return valid directions but no exposure or spatial metrics.

- **Building shade is an estimate, not a measurement.** The flat-roof prism model ignores facade geometry, pitched roofs, terrain elevation, street furniture, and building height measurement error. One record in the supplied footprint dataset is invalid and silently skipped, introducing a small blind spot near that location.

- **Tree canopy coverage is unavailable.** The 2025 PPR Tree Inventory provides trunk point locations only — no crown outlines. The app reports a count of trunks within 10 m of each route. True canopy coverage percentage requires polygon data not yet present in the dataset.

- **Exposure metrics reflect open-sky conditions, not sidewalk-level.** `sunExposurePercent` is based on Open-Meteo Direct Normal Irradiance for an unobstructed sky. When building shade data is available the figure is scaled down proportionally, but localized canyon effects, street furniture, and terrain are not modeled.

- **Weather reflects current conditions, not an arrival forecast.** Route temperature and precipitation values are interpolated from _current_ readings along the path, not a prediction of conditions at your future arrival time. The 12-sample exposure forecast is the closest approximation to a future-conditions estimate.

- **Wind is modeled at 10 m height.** Forecast wind speed excludes street-level shelter, wind tunnels between buildings, and localized urban microclimate effects.

- **Rain exposure ignores overhead shelter.** Covered bus stops, awnings, and building overhangs are not in the model; `rainExposurePercent` will overstate exposure on routes with significant overhead cover.

- **No real-time GPS tracking or turn-by-turn navigation.** Live progress tracking and ElevenLabs voice guidance were planned but are deferred beyond the hackathon scope. Routes are static from the moment they are fetched.

- **Alternative route quality.** When Mapbox returns fewer than three routes, the backend synthesizes alternatives via waypoint detours. These may not be meaningfully distinct, and any detour exceeding 2.2× the direct distance is discarded, so fewer than three routes is always possible.

- **Snowfall units are not merged.** Open-Meteo and OpenWeather report snow using different units and accumulation windows; the two values remain separate in the API response.

- **No offline support.** All route, weather, and search requests require an active connection to both the backend server and upstream API providers.
